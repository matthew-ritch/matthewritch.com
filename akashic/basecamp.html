Specification sources

  • 1 Basecamp organizes work around projects — each project has its own message board, to-do lists, file storage, schedule, and group chat (Campfire). The goal is to avoid tool sprawl: one place for everything related to a piece of work
  • 2 Teams are either scattered across too many tools (half in GitHub issues, half in Slack, decisions lost in email threads) or suffer from the opposite extreme: a heavy Scrum or Jira process that generates overhead without progress
  • 3 Basecamp — $299/month flat, unlimited users. No per-seat pricing. This is explicitly positioned as anti-enterprise-SaaS: one price, everyone in, no negotiation
  • 4 Software product teams at established companies — particularly those burned by Jira's complexity or the chaos of Slack + email + spreadsheet coordination
  • 5 one place for everything related to a piece of work, rather than decisions scattered across Slack, Notion, Jira, and email
  • 6 Message board — For announcements, decisions, and discussions that don't need to be real-time. Posts have full formatting and can be commented on
  • 7 To-do lists — Created by the team, not assigned top-down. Tasks are grouped into named lists. Anyone can create to-dos
  • 8 Campfire (group chat) — Per-project chat room for loose, real-time communication. Not the same as a company-wide Slack channel; scoped to the people working on the project
  • 9 Schedule — Milestone dates and events relevant to the project, visible to the whole team
  • 10 Hey! and Pings — The Hey! menu centralizes all notifications
  • 11 Neither produces the feeling of 'making it' — shipping meaningful work on a consistent cadence
  • 12 Four steps: Set boundaries → Rough out elements → Address risks and rabbit holes → Write the pitch
  • 13 Basecamp markets itself to teams that want fewer meetings, less process overhead, and a clear cadence of meaningful shipments rather than an endless stream of micro-tasks

Reality sources

  • ▣ 1 Homepage shows 'Lineup' navigation for project schedules and 'Your Schedule' and 'Your Assignments' sections
  • 2 Task creation requires navigating to project, then To-dos, then New list with multiple form fields
  • 3 Pings section shows 'You don't have any Pings yet' suggesting structured rather than instant communication
  • 4 Lineup page shows 'Scheduled projects' focused on project timelines, not calendar appointments
  • 5 Docs & Files appears as tab within project context, not standalone document management
  • ▣ 6 Homepage prominently displays project list with sample projects showing Message Board, To-dos, Docs & Files structure
  • 7 Sample projects 'Getting Started' and 'Making a Podcast' demonstrate typical Basecamp project organization
  • 8 Clean layout with organized sections and clear project boundaries creates structured workspace feeling
  • 9 Communication happens through Message Board and Pings rather than chat streams, with structured project contexts
